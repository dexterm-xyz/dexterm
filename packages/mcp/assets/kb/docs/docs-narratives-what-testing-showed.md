We replayed 11 days of dexterm's recorded history (219,730 coins) through the tracker in time order, reading each
coin's narrative at the first moment a scanner could act on it (point in time: nothing recorded after that moment),
and tested it on days that were held back until the end, the same way as dexterm's other entry research.

- **It is a useful map of what is running, not an edge.** On the held-back days, adding the narrative to a coin's own
  numbers did not help tell apart the coins that later tripled (ranking quality fell slightly, by 0.022 (95% interval -0.059 to +0.008)) or the ones that drained
  (fell by 0.033 (-0.052 to -0.015)).
- **None of the narrative gates we fixed in advance improved paper results** when added to the existing entry rules:
  joining an igniting narrative early, skipping the 5th or later copy of a saturated narrative, and skipping fading or
  dead narratives. In the 22 combinations of these gates and rules with at least 20 paper entries, none raised the average result per entry and three lowered it. (One more gate, added after looking at the earlier days, is reported apart; its 5 combinations moved nothing either way.) Every gate also skipped some of the coins that went on to run.
- **What it does describe:** a coin that is part of a narrative (two or more coins) drained within 6 hours about twice as often as a coin on its own (about 50% against 27% on the held-back days), and also tripled a little more often. Copy-cat waves are where both the runs and the drains happen. The lifecycle states themselves did not sort the outcomes: the share that later tripled was 4% to 6% in every state.
- In a hand-graded sample of 100 narratives, 70 were coherent (one meme and its copies, or one theme) and the
  label named the theme in 87. The usual mistakes: the same ticker reused for an unrelated coin, and a shared
  generic word.

Every number above comes with its sample size and a 95% interval in the study; the narratives table is a way to see
what is running, and the numbers say it should not be read as a reason to trade.
